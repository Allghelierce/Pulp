PULP — TIMER + RECALL DESIGN
Topics as trees: focus plants them, remembering grows them
============================================================


THE CORE IDEA
-------------
Every tree in the orchard is a TOPIC you studied.

  - The FOCUS TIMER plants the tree and grows it to a SAPLING.
  - RECALL (answering questions about that topic) grows it the rest of
    the way into a FULL TREE.
  - Only full trees produce sap.

The orchard becomes a map of what you know: full trees are topics you've
mastered, saplings are topics you still owe a review. It works like a
memory palace — tying an idea to a place and an object you can see makes
it much easier to remember.


WHY KEEP THE TIMER AT ALL
-------------------------
Recall only measures one kind of studying: remembering what you've
already written. Most study time is something else:

  - Learning new material (reading, lectures, first-pass notes) — nothing
    to quiz yet.
  - Work outside Pulp (problem sets on paper, essays, coding, practice
    exams).
  - Brand-new users with an empty notebook.

Timer = learning (time in). Recall = remembering (proof it stuck).
Together they cover both halves of studying.


GROWTH STAGES (no new art needed)
---------------------------------
The sprites have 4 visual stages. The split maps onto them directly:

  Stage 0-1   Seed -> sprout     grown by the timer
  Stage 2     Sapling            grown by the timer (the timer's ceiling)
  Stage 3     Full tree          grown by RECALL

How much recall a tree needs scales with rarity, the same way the timer
already takes longer for rare trees (e.g. a tangerine ~5 correct answers,
a rare tree ~15).


HOW IT FEELS IN CLASS (no interruptions)
----------------------------------------
1. During a focus session: the timer runs and nothing else happens.
   No popups, no questions.
2. Session ends: one tap. The AI reads what you wrote during the session,
   names the topic (e.g. "Photosynthesis"), plants a sapling with that
   name, and quietly turns the new notes into recall cards tagged to that
   topic. The only sign is a small "5 cards queued" line — never a quiz.
3. Later, whenever the student chooses: they recall, and the topic's
   sapling grows into a full tree.

Why waiting isn't a compromise: recalling material hours or days later
produces stronger memory than quizzing right away (the spacing effect).
Deferring recall is better for learning, not just for the classroom.


TOPICS AND TREES
----------------
- Each tree = one topic. The AI picks the main topic of a session; one
  topic per tree keeps it simple.
- Cards belong to topics. Recalling photosynthesis cards grows the
  Photosynthesis tree, not some random one.
- Tap a tree to review it. Tapping a sapling in the orchard offers
  "Review Photosynthesis". Going back to the tree to remember the idea is
  where the memory-palace effect comes from.
- Studying the same topic again plants a NEW tree next to the first one.
  The orchard already groups trees into clusters, so a topic becomes a
  small grove — a bigger grove means you've studied it more.
- Topic names show on hover only, so the orchard doesn't get cluttered.


SHOWING HOW FRESH A TOPIC IS
----------------------------
A tree's color shows how fresh that topic is in your memory right now.

  - Freshly reviewed: full, vivid color.
  - As the topic's cards come due for review (spaced repetition), the tree
    slowly fades: color drains gradually the longer reviews sit overdue.
  - Reviewing the topic brings the color back, with a short animation.

Rules:
  - Visual only. Fading never reduces sap, growth, or size. It informs,
    it doesn't punish.
  - There's a floor: a fully faded tree is muted but still clearly
    visible, never grey or dead-looking.
  - A faded tree is a natural nudge: tap it -> "Review <topic>".
  - Freshness comes from the topic's cards: if none are due, the tree is
    full color; the more overdue they are, the more it fades.

How it's built: a CSS color filter on the existing sprite (saturation and
brightness), so no new art.

Considered and skipped for now: bigger trees for mastery, a glow for
fully mastered topics, flowers at the base, and varying fruit count (the
last one would mean redrawing every species).


WHAT HAPPENS IN THE EDGE CASES
------------------------------
Notes written WITHOUT the timer:
  The notes still become recall cards and get a topic. You can review
  them. But the topic has no tree until a focus session plants one.

Recall when no sapling is waiting for that topic:
  Correct answers are BANKED as nutrients for that topic. When a focus
  session plants that topic's sapling, it absorbs the banked growth and
  can jump straight to full. Recall is never wasted, and it gives students
  a reason to start the timer. (Recall also still pays a little sap.)

A focus session where nothing was written in Pulp (studying on paper):
  That sapling has no cards of its own, so it would be stuck. Fix: let
  due cards from that notebook (or any notebook) finish it.

Trees students already have:
  Trees already grown fully on the timer stay fully grown. Only new trees
  follow the new rules.


REMINDERS, NOT INTERRUPTIONS
----------------------------
- A small due count on the focus button.
- Spaced repetition brings cards back on later days; later reviews pay
  bonus sap.
- Nothing is ever taken away. Trees never wilt or shrink for skipping
  recall — saplings just don't produce sap until they're grown.
- Optional quiet mode for class hours: hides the due count and reminders
  entirely.
- Optional "quick check now?" at the end of a session, off by default,
  for people studying at home.


ALREADY BUILT (feature/recall-mode branch)
------------------------------------------
- Typed answers + AI grading: students type the answer from memory; the
  AI marks it correct / partly right / not quite, with one line of
  feedback and the expected answer shown.
- Growth comes from the AI's verdict, not the button clicked, so wrong
  answers can't be farmed.
- Correct answers grow trees (this will be reworked to finish saplings
  under the new design).


BUILD ORDER
-----------
1. Topic tagging: at session end, the AI names the session's topic from
   the notes written during it, and its cards are tagged to that topic.
2. Growth split: the timer caps at sapling; recall on a topic's cards
   grows that topic's sapling to a full tree.
3. Banked nutrients: recall with no sapling waiting banks growth for the
   topic; a new sapling absorbs it.
4. Orchard: topic names on hover, tap a sapling -> "Review <topic>".
5. Only full trees produce sap; existing trees keep their growth.
6. Freshness: trees fade as their topic's reviews come due, and regain
   color when reviewed.
7. Due count on the focus button, quiet mode, optional quick check.
